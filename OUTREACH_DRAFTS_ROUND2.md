## What changed, and why the DMs read the way they do

The first drafts were emails pasted into a DM box: four tidy paragraphs, a rule of
three, a punchline closer. Melchi rejected them, correctly. What replaced them:

1. **Say what the app does BEFORE the shop-specific hook.** Every early draft opened
   with a compliment about the shop and then pivoted to an unrelated pitch. That is
   the most recognisable cold-sales move there is, and it reads worse than no hook,
   because it signals someone who did thirty seconds of research. The hook only works
   once the reader knows what is being offered, so it can function as a *reason*
   instead of flattery.
2. **The hook has to actually connect to the offer.** An early line claimed a town's
   rescue fundraiser was why he was writing "and not to a chain". Those two facts have
   nothing to do with each other, and he caught it. Test every "which is why".
3. **Never write an anti-chain line.** He plans to pitch chains later, and franchisees
   frequently can approve their own store.
4. **Only claims he can defend.** He asked whether to just say he had visited these
   shops. No. Every specific below comes from press or the shop's own site.

evidence. **Teadori nearly closed in September 2023**: sales were low, they were
