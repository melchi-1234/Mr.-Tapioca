For boba shops

Students focus for four hours. The only place to collect is your counter.

Mr. Tapioca is a study timer. A student blocks the apps that distract them (on iPhone, using Apple's Screen Time), works, and a little cup of boba fills up while they do. When the cup is full, a partner shop is the reward. You pick what that reward is, in your own words, and you can pull it any day you like.

This page is the honest version. It says what works today, what is coming in the next release, and what this will never be able to measure.

What the student actually does Live today

1

Puts the phone down

On iPhone the app shields their distracting apps for the length of the session, using Apple's own Screen Time controls. They pick which apps. It is a real block, not a tamper proof one: iOS lets a student clear their own limits for the day, and reinstalling a blocked app drops it out until they pick it again.

2

Studies

One session runs anywhere from 15 minutes to 4 hours. A tapioca pearl character brews their drink on screen while the timer runs.

3

Fills the cup

Minutes add up across days, not in one sitting. Four hours of focus in total is the bar today. It is one number shared by the whole partner list, not a setting each shop holds. The fine print says what that means.

4

Walks in

The reward only exists in person, at your counter. There is no online order, no code emailed anywhere, and nothing to ship.

What the shop provides Live today

  • One offer, written by you. A percentage, a free topping, a size upgrade, a set amount off. Whatever you would actually be happy to hand over. We copy your wording into the app exactly as you send it.
  • Almost nothing else. No hardware, no register integration, no app for your staff to install, no account to create, no fee to join and no minimum term. The one thing we do ask for is your offer in writing, in your own words, so that neither of us is going on memory about what you agreed to. An email saying it is enough. That is the whole paperwork.
  • You honor it in person. A student who has done the hours shows the reward at the counter and your cashier applies it. That is the whole transaction.

The offer is yours, including the off switch Live today on the web

  • You set the words and the number. We never write an offer on your behalf and we never round a number up to make it sound better. If you say five percent, the app says five percent.
  • You change it whenever you want. Send new wording and it replaces the old one. There is no approval step and no minimum term.
  • You leave the web app the same day you ask. Pulling a shop is one line removed from one file. It is not a build, not a release and not a meeting. Phones are slower, and one case is slower than the rest: a phone that has not been online in a while, or is on an older App Store build, keeps showing the copy of the list it already has. The fine print at the bottom says exactly how slow that gets and what we tell you about it.

At the counter

How it works today Live

The student opens your card inside the app. It shows your shop name, your offer in your words, and a clock that ticks forward while the card is open.

The honest limit: that card is a card in an app, and it is not proof of anything. It opens at full strength even for a student who has earned nothing, with only the confirm button switched off. Closing it with "Not now" spends nothing, so a student who earns one reward can show the same full card at your counter again the next day, and the day after. Your cashier has no way to check it against anything. That is not us being modest. It is the exact reason the next release exists.

What replaces it Next release

The student shows a short code instead of a card. Your cashier types it into a plain web page on their own phone, and the page answers in one word: valid, or already used.

Nothing to install. No account, no login, no staff training. One code is good for one use, and it is marked used at the moment your cashier accepts it, not before.

What you will be able to see Next release

None of this is live today. Today a redemption never leaves the student's phone, so there is no report to give you and we do not give you one. The list below arrives with the counter code above, because the code is what creates the record in the first place.

  • Redemptions. How many times your offer was accepted at your counter.
  • Separate accounts. How many different accounts that was, which is not the same as how many different people. An account is a random string the app makes on the device. It carries no name, no email and no phone number, so we have nothing like that to give you and would not hand it over if we did. Accounts are also free, so one person could hold two. The report says accounts rather than people for exactly that reason.
  • Repeat redeemers. How many of those accounts came back and did the hours again for a second one.
  • Which offer was live. The exact wording that was running when each redemption happened, so changing your offer does not blur the history.
  • When they happened. By day and by hour, which is the number that tells you whether this lands in your slow window or your rush.
  • Rejected attempts. Codes your cashier typed in that came back refused, and the reason: already used, expired, or not a real code.

What we will never be able to tell you

These are not on a roadmap. They are outside what this can know.

Mr. Tapioca never touches your register. It does not see an order, a total, or a card. So the following numbers are not hard for us, they are impossible, and any version of them we handed you would be a guess wearing a chart.

  • Revenue. We never see a transaction, so we cannot report one.
  • Incremental sales. We cannot tell you whether a student would have walked in anyway. Nobody who does not run your register can.
  • First-time visitors. We do not know who has been in your shop before. A student who has been coming for a year looks identical to one who has never been in.
  • Average order value. We never see the order, only that the offer was accepted.
  • Return on investment. It is built out of the four above, so it is not available either.

If you ever hear us claim one of these, push back and make us show the number. An earlier draft of this pitch did claim two of them. That is why they are listed here by name.

The fine print, on purpose

  • We do not call this verified study time. The app counts minutes from the phone's own clock and keeps them on the phone. It is a good faith measure of somebody sitting down and working, and it is not proof. Making it something a shop could actually lean on is the point of the next release, and until that ships we will not use the word verified.
  • The four hour bar is one shared number, not a setting each shop holds. Both current partners are recorded at four hours, so today it makes no practical difference. The part worth knowing: when the app works out whether a student is eligible, it takes the lowest bar on the whole partner list and applies it to every offer, including yours. If a third shop ever signed on at two hours, two hours would become the bar for your offer too. Whether shops should get their own bar is a decision that has not been made yet, and we would tell you which way it went before you signed on.
  • Adding or pulling a shop reaches the web app the same day. It reaches the iPhone app with the next App Store release, which is a real gap of days, not minutes. Two slower cases are worth naming, because you should hear them from us and not from a customer. The list a phone has already downloaded does not expire on its own: it is replaced the next time that phone opens the shop map inside the app with a working connection, so a phone that stays offline keeps the old list for as long as it stays offline. And underneath that sits a shop list built into the app itself, which is what a fresh install with no signal falls back to, and that one only changes with an App Store release. If you ask to come off, we tell you the day it is off the web app and the day it is off phones, separately, and we say plainly that the long tail above is not something we can chase down device by device.
  • Everything on this page marked next release is written and not shipped. It exists as code and tests. It has not run against real students at a real counter. When it has, this page changes and the chips move from next release to live.
