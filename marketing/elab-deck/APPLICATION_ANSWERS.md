**Company/Team Name**

Mr. Tapioca

**One-Sentence Business Summary**

Mr. Tapioca is a boba-themed study timer, live on the App Store, where finishing long focus sessions earns students real discounts at partner boba shops.

**What customer problem does your business solve? (100 words max)**

Students lose study hours to their phones, and the apps meant to fix it reward you with pixels. Forest has 60 million users growing virtual trees, which proves people will focus for a reward, but a virtual tree stops mattering by week two. Meanwhile the boba shops around every campus are fighting for student foot traffic with punch cards and Instagram ads. We connect the two: students study with real app blocking, and finishing a multi-hour drink earns a discount they redeem in person at a nearby shop. The reward is real, so the habit sticks.

*(96 words)*

**Who is your early adopter customer?**

Our early adopter is the college student who studies in long blocks and already buys boba weekly. Think of a sophomore pre-med in the library with a six-hour problem set, phone face down, who walks past three bubble tea shops on the way home. She already pays for focus apps and for $7 drinks; we let one pay for the other. On the shop side, the early adopter is the independent boba shop within walking distance of campus, run by an owner who knows regulars by name and whose marketing is a punch card and an Instagram account.

**What customer discovery have you done, and how did it influence your business?**

Our discovery so far has come from shipping to real users, and it has changed the product three times. First, we designed drinks as single sittings until it was obvious nobody studies six hours straight, so we rebuilt the timer so a Large drink banks progress across multiple sessions. Second, early users (including Apple's own App Review team) could not find the app blocking feature, so we moved it into a prompt on the Start Focus button. Third, our first paying customers hit a bug within days of launch where purchased skins could not be equipped, and we shipped the fix in about a day. What we have not done yet is structured discovery with shop owners: whether they will fund a reward for verified student walk-ins is the question our business depends on, and it is exactly what we want to spend eLab's fall answering. We have started through Launchpad Ezra. Mentor Doug Allan, who spent thirty years in food and beverage sales, walked me through the margin math a shop owner runs on a discount (a 10% discount can be half the profit on a drink), and we redesigned our pilot around it: rewards that trigger only on a student's first visit to a shop, in-kind rewards like a free topping instead of percent discounts, and a capped pilot the owner can measure. I also have calls booked with mentor Krishna Sasank Talasila on customer acquisition and with Felix Litvinsky.

**How do customers currently solve this problem?**

Students use Forest, Flora, or Opal, or just put the phone in another room. Those apps either charge subscriptions or pay out in virtual coins and trees, and the reward loses its pull once the novelty fades. Nothing they earn exists outside the screen. Shops, on the other side, buy Instagram ads, run punch cards, and pay delivery-app fees, none of which reach a student at the exact moment they have earned a treat and are deciding where to take a study break. The two sides are solving their halves of the problem separately; nobody connects them.

**Have you formed an entity, raised capital, or generated revenue?**

No entity and no outside capital. The app is live on the App Store (apps.apple.com/app/id6786023560) and has its first revenue: small one-time cosmetic purchases, starting in launch week. We would incorporate once the partner model proves out.
